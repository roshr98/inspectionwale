RC Transfer Process in Maharashtra: Step-by-Step

How to transfer used-car ownership at the RTO — forms, documents, fees, NOC rules and the mistakes that delay your transfer.

Home › Blog › RC Transfer Maharashtra

Buying the car is only half the job. Until the Registration Certificate (RC) is transferred into your name, you are not the legal owner — and the seller remains liable for the vehicle. This guide explains the RC transfer process at Maharashtra RTOs in plain language, including the forms, documents, fees and timelines.

This article is a practical overview for general guidance. Exact fees and procedures can change and vary by RTO. Always confirm current requirements on the official Parivahan portal or with your local RTO before submitting.

When does ownership transfer apply?

A transfer of ownership is needed whenever a registered vehicle changes hands — a normal sale between two people, a sale through a dealer, or transfer due to inheritance or auction. The most common case is a straightforward private sale within the same RTO jurisdiction, which is what this guide focuses on.

Documents you'll need

From the seller

  • Original Registration Certificate (RC).
  • Form 29 (notice of transfer of ownership) — two copies, signed.
  • Form 30 (report of transfer) — signed.
  • Valid insurance certificate.
  • Valid PUC (pollution) certificate.
  • If the car had a loan: Form 35 and the bank's NOC confirming the hypothecation is cleared.
  • Seller's PAN (or Form 60) and address/identity proof.

From the buyer

  • Identity and address proof (Aadhaar, passport, etc.).
  • Passport-size photographs.
  • PAN card.
  • Fresh insurance in the buyer's name (or transferred policy).

Step-by-step transfer process

  1. Clear any loan first. If the car was financed, get the hypothecation removed (Form 35 + bank NOC) before or alongside the transfer.
  2. Get insurance sorted. Transfer the existing policy to your name or take a fresh policy. Driving an untransferred policy can void claims.
  3. Fill Forms 29 and 30. Both buyer and seller sign. Form 29 is submitted in duplicate.
  4. Apply on Parivahan / at the RTO. Many steps can be initiated online via the Vahan/Parivahan portal; the application and document upload start there, with a slot at the RTO where required.
  5. Pay the transfer fee. Pay the prescribed ownership-transfer fee and any smart-card charge. Keep the receipt.
  6. Submit documents and verification. The RTO verifies the documents; physical inspection of the vehicle may be required in some cases.
  7. Receive the updated RC. Once processed, the RC is reissued (smart card) in the buyer's name and dispatched. Verify the details on Parivahan once updated.

Fees and timelines

The ownership-transfer fee for a private car is modest, with an additional smart-card charge; agents typically add a service fee if you use one. Processing commonly takes a couple of weeks but can vary by RTO workload and whether physical verification is needed. Treat any quoted timeline as an estimate and follow up using your application number.

Inter-RTO and inter-state transfers

If the vehicle is moving to a different RTO or another state, additional steps apply — typically an NOC from the original RTO, re-registration at the new RTO, and in inter-state cases, road-tax considerations and possible refund of tax from the previous state. These cases take longer and are worth handling carefully or via a reliable agent.

Common mistakes that delay transfer

  • Forgetting to remove an old loan/hypothecation before applying.
  • Incomplete or unsigned Form 29/30.
  • Expired PUC or insurance at the time of submission.
  • Mismatch between the RC details and the physical chassis/engine numbers.
  • Paying the seller in full before the transfer is even initiated.

Protect yourself before you transfer

The transfer makes you the legal owner of whatever you actually bought — so make sure that's a sound car. Verify that the chassis and engine numbers physically match the RC, confirm there's no outstanding loan, and ideally have the car inspected before money changes hands. A pre-purchase inspection also confirms the identity details that the RTO will later verify.
